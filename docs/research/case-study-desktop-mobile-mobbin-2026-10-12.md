# Case study research: 20 desktop + 20 mobile examples

Moriz Studio · 12 October 2026

Use an image-led opening with a compact project overview, then explain the challenge, two or three design decisions, the delivered solution and a documented outcome. Koto is the closest studio reference; Attio supplies a useful synopsis, Nite Riot makes roles explicit, and Lightdash shows how to connect verified outcomes to an editorial story.

## Scope and sources

This set contains **20 desktop references and 20 responsive mobile views across 20 distinct case studies from 19 publishers**. It includes 3 creative/production projects, 1 design-team study and 16 customer stories; customer stories provide adjacent narrative patterns for Moriz, not examples of design-process documentation.

Desktop images come from Mobbin. Mobile images come from the same original websites, rendered at 390 × 1100 on 12 October 2026. They are real responsive captures, not resized desktop images or native-app screens. Mobbin capture dates are unknown. Several sites have redesigned their pages; the pairing is by case study, not necessarily by identical content or layout.

## Recommended Moriz case study structure

1. **Opening:** project name, a short plain-language description and one strong product image. Include a clear route back to all projects.
2. **Overview:** client, year, studio role, collaborators and services. Keep facts distinct from the narrative.
3. **Challenge:** who needed what, what was difficult and the constraints. Use a short paragraph rather than generic process language.
4. **Decisions:** two or three consequential choices. For each, explain the reason and show an annotated screen or comparison.
5. **Delivered solution:** a coherent sequence of real interfaces, relevant video or image pairs. Supply useful captions.
6. **Outcome:** documented result, client feedback or a clear account of what shipped. Never invent usage numbers, research findings or business impact.
7. **Continue:** next project, all projects and a direct WhatsApp enquiry about a similar project.

## Responsive and Hebrew adaptation

- Preserve the meaning of the desktop hierarchy in a single reading column. Put project context near the first image rather than leaving it in a desktop sidebar.
- Use Moriz’s Leon body font and established display typography. Start body copy around 18–20px and check actual Hebrew wrapping.
- Stack two-image blocks when necessary. Keep UI screenshots readable and allow opening a larger version; do not crop away the feature being explained.
- Keep essential narrative open. Use disclosure controls for optional technical or process detail.
- Prefer a compact chapter control to several accumulating sticky bars. Check overlap with the existing hide-on-scroll header.
- Mirror directional navigation for RTL. Keep English product names and numbers readable with appropriate direction isolation.

## Content needed before implementation

For each Moriz project, collect the approved client name, date, studio contribution, starting problem, constraints, two or three decisions with supporting screens, delivered scope and any publishable outcome or quote. Use the existing CMS’s ordered text, image, two-image, gallery and video blocks. A richer case study should begin with verified content; these examples do not justify fabricating a design process.

## Exclusions and limits

The initial live checks rejected retired Webflow/Shift, Intercom/Amplitude, Zendesk/Stanley, ClickUp/STANLEY, Vanta/SmartRecruiters and Stripe/Adore Me URLs that returned errors or redirected to collections. Canva/HubSpot was also excluded because its mobile page blocked automated viewing. Their screenshots are not counted. The manifest records the checks. A separate query log retains search metadata; the final 20 retained section previews were checked visually and only those references are included in the board.

Visual precedent research, not customer interviews or usability testing. Current live websites may differ from Mobbin’s archived desktop captures. Screenshots do not establish motion, accessibility, performance or conversion effectiveness.

Research files only; no production website changes were made for this request.
