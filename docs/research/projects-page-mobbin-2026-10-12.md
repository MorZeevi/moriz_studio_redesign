# Projects page — Mobbin research

Research date: 12–13 October 2026. Purpose: the dedicated `/he/projects` index for Moriz Studio, covering the hero, category filters, a layout switcher, project cards with an information reveal and a contact card inside the grid. It builds on the [11 October project presentation research](projects-presentation-mobbin-2026-10-11.md), which covers the homepage panels and case-study pages and is not repeated here.

That study advised against filters and view switches *for three homepage projects*. The owner has now asked for both on the dedicated index, which is expected to grow. The guidance below therefore makes the controls quiet, keeps them working with a single project, and keeps the contact route available in every state.

## Key findings

1. **Studio indexes lead with media and keep text secondary.** In Koto, Fiasco and Instrument the cover takes most of the tile, and the name and one value line or tag line sit outside or on top of the image. This is an established pattern for visual portfolios. It is not a measured conversion result.
2. **Filters read best as typography rather than as UI.** MOUTHWASH sets its Service / Industry filters as a short typographic list, and Instrument uses tiny pills labelled "Recent work". Kajabi's dropdowns, search box and "Reset all" read as a catalogue tool, which is the dashboard feel the brief rules out.
3. **The meaning of a layout change is in its label.** MOUTHWASH's index view has a "View" slider, and the Studio Freight chrome shows "Grid / List / Zoom" as plain words. In both cases the switch is a quiet text control next to the filters, never a toolbar.
4. **An editorial rhythm comes from deliberate pairs.** Fiasco offsets a large tile against a smaller one in alternate rows. Koto mixes tall and short tiles in two columns. Both compositions repeat, so they read as intentional rather than random.
5. **The contact route sits where browsing ends.** Koto puts "Want to discuss a project? Get in touch" beside the next project. Fiasco and Vucko use one oversized question with a single understated action. None of the retained references place a CTA tile inside the grid, so that placement is a **hypothesis** to test (see below).

## Recommended direction

| Area | Recommendation | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Large Danidin heading with a red superscript live project count and one Leon line. No hero media; the grid is the visual. | A24-style typographic index; established hierarchy principle |
| Controls | Outlined text-like pills with counts on the start side and a three-word layout switch on the end side. The bar becomes sticky once the grid scrolls. | MOUTHWASH, Instrument; Kajabi as the counter-example |
| Layout | Standard 2-column, Compact 3-column at wide screens, Editorial as a repeating 12-column pattern of pairs (8/4, 12 wide, 5/7, 4/4/4). Featured projects take the large slots. | Fiasco, Koto |
| Card | Media first. On hover and on keyboard focus, a dark 80% panel rises with the title, type, one-line value and services, and the image scales slightly. Touch screens show the title, type and one line permanently below the media. The whole card is the link, with no "View project" button. | Established: whole-card target, focus/hover parity, no hover dependence on touch |
| Contact | A brand-red tile in the grid — "אולי הפרויקט הבא הוא שלך?" — with one button to the footer contact form. It stays in the sequence for every filter, including an empty result. The footer form is the closing CTA. | Koto, Fiasco, Vucko for tone; the grid placement is a hypothesis |

## Usability and conversion

**Established principles applied**

- The whole card is a single link with a descriptive accessible name. Hover and `:focus-visible` reveal the same information.
- Touch layouts show essential information without hover.
- Filters are real buttons with a pressed state. The result count is announced politely, and the URL reflects the filter so a filtered view can be shared.
- An empty filter result explains itself and offers a way back to all projects.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` removes movement but keeps the information.
- No layout shift: the saved layout is applied before paint, and every media frame reserves its aspect ratio.

**Hypotheses, unverified**

- A contact tile after the third project draws enquiries without disrupting browsing.
- Giving featured projects larger tiles moves attention to the strongest work.
- The editorial layout is a better default than the standard grid.

Measure these with real traffic, for example clicks on the contact tile versus the footer form, before treating them as findings.

## Method and limits

Five Mobbin searches were run: four website-section searches and one web-screen search. They returned 48 placements. Every preview was inspected, and eleven sections from seven sites were retained and downloaded at full resolution. The web-screen search returned product galleries (Dribbble, Behance, Savee and others) rather than studio indexes, so none were retained.

Several studio sections rendered blank or showed only the intro frame in Mobbin's capture: OFF+BRAND, Phantom Studios, Analogue Agency, Studio Freight and Aino Agency. They are listed in the manifest and not used as evidence. Studio Freight's visible chrome ("Grid / List / Zoom") is cited only as a label pattern.

Mobbin captures static frames, so no hover or transition behaviour could be observed. The hover and focus reveal is a design decision, not a borrowed pattern. No claim is made about the conversion performance of any reference.
